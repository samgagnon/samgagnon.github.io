Shareholder Primacy

by Samuel Gagnon-Hartman
21 October 2024

Boeing and GE aren't what they used to be. With every passing year, it seems that the quality of goods and services provided by American giants deteriorates. What is the root case? Many have on the right have pointed to DEI initiatives and HR departments as the cause of the decline. They argue that the focus on diversity and inclusion has distracted from the core mission of the company. However, I find that this argument does not survive contact with evidence. [ADD EVIDENCE] The real problem is the focus on shareholder primacy. Shareholder primacy is the idea that the only goal of a company is to maximize shareholder value. This idea has been the dominant ideology in the United States since the 1980s. It has led to a focus on short-term profits at the expense of long-term growth and sustainability. [ADD EVIDENCE] We ought to expand the above to include graphics and a narrativized retelling of the rise and fall of the American corporation. What is a public entity? What is a private entity? What is the role of the state in the economy? What do corporations owe to the rest of us, if anything? I want to tie this into my theory on the Professional Managerial Class's seizure of USian institutions, and how this sets the Anglosphere apart from the pensioner-states of Europe. [ADD EVIDENCE] In a free-enterprise, private-property system, a corporate executive is an employee of the owners of the business. He has direct responsibility to his employers. That responsibility is to conduct the business in accordance with their desires ... the key point is that, in his capacity as a corporate executive, the manager is the agent of the individuals who own the corporation ... and his primary responsibility is to them. — Milton Friedman. "A Friedman Doctrine: The Social Responsibility of Business Is to Increase Its Profits". The New York Times. September 13, 1970.[9] Under the doctrine outlined above, the manager is an employee of the shareholders. We may therefore say that the timescale relevant to management is identical to the timescale relevant to the shareholders. If shareholders are short-termists, i.e., traders, then the business only cares about short-term performance. If shareholders are concerned with long-term performance, then the business will be concerned with long-term performance. We should investigate the holdings of Warren Buffet and his personal letters. Did he own GE? What did he say about its decline?
